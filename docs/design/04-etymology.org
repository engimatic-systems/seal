#+title: Etymology: Following, Sign, and Seal

* Scope

This is a design note, not a claim that Seal's runtime contract follows from an
etymology. The historical connections are useful because they name the relation
Seal must preserve: later acts must follow from earlier, authorized acts.

* Following and consequence

The Latin family around /sequor/ means "to follow" (Claiborne 1989, 210). It
gives English a cluster of words that describe Seal's reference trace:

- a /sequence/ is an ordered following;
- a /consequence/ is what follows with an act; and
- to /execute/ a plan is, in its Latin history, to follow it out.

Seal's trace is therefore not only chronological:

#+begin_example
proposal -> observation -> authorization -> execution -> result
#+end_example

Each arrow is a claim of consequence. The execution must follow the observed,
authorized proposal. The result must follow the actual execution. A later
artifact that merely resembles its predecessor does not satisfy the contract.

* Sign, standard, and seal

One proposed account derives Latin /signum/ from a form meaning "standard that
one follows," related to the same Proto-Indo-European root (Watkins 2011).
Under that account, a sign or seal is a visible mark that later action follows.

This derivation is contested. Another account relates /signum/ to a root meaning
"to cut," through a carved or cut-out mark (de Vaan 2008). Seal does not depend
on resolving that historical question. Both readings are semantically apt:

- a seal marks and holds a boundary around a consequential object; and
- a seal provides a visible standard that the host may follow into action.

The name should not imply magic containment. Seal does not bind an agent or
make arbitrary approved code safe. It binds host-side local execution to the
operator's terms. The agent remains free to receive a refusal and author a new
proposal.

* Operational reading

Seal holds an agent-proposed act still, exposes its terms, and admits it into
local execution only under human judgment.

The host must follow the seal mechanically. The human must not follow a proposal
blindly. That distinction is the point of the review boundary.
